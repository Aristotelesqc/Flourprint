# Readiness criteria: claims to verify, and a catalogue of signs

**Status.** Research findings and questions for the design, written on 2026-11-14, 2026-11-15 and
2026-11-16 (UTC) under issues #231 and #287. Documentation only: nothing here is a decision, and no
model, coefficient, contract, engine or screen changes. Nothing is added to a "Decided" list. A
proposed ADR (0025, pull request #296, status Proposed, not accepted) would make the fermentation
engine a planning tool that does not judge readiness, put the end criterion in the step of a recipe
and in the baker's judgement, and have the product carry a codified body of baking knowledge; it
leaves the form of a step's criterion and the record format of a knowledge entry open. This document
is material for that body, and neither assumes the ADR nor decides what it leaves open.

**Where the claims come from.** The twelve claims below (C1 to C12) are the owner's practice as a
home baker. The owner submitted them for verification, said plainly that his word is not
authoritative, and asked that each be checked against craft knowledge (reference works, trade
references, professional bodies, manufacturers, recipe sources) and against measured science. They
are recorded as "owner's practice", never as a source. Where a source contradicts him, this document
says so.

**How to read it.** Section order: what was read; the twelve claims (table, then one paragraph each);
the three most contested criteria; what the repository's recipes record; the catalogue of signs by
stage; the synthesis and the questions for design; coverage and gaps; a proposed data shape; and a
table that ties every register entry to the rows that use it. A sign's level (A, B or C) is the level
of its register entry in [craft-knowledge.md](craft-knowledge.md); a reading of a scientific source is
in [sources.md](sources.md#classification-of-evidence). A statement resting on a page that was not read
here is marked as a lead and is never used as a finding.

## How a verdict is worded

| Verdict          | Meaning                                                                              |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| supported        | At least one source read says it, with its conditions, and none read contradicts it. |
| partly supported | One half, the mechanism or a limited case is supported; the nuance is stated.        |
| qualified        | Supported in a form narrower than the claim, or with a disagreement among sources.   |
| contradicted     | A source read says the opposite under comparable conditions.                         |
| no source read   | Nothing readable bears on it. This is not "false".                                   |

### C1. "Doubled once" almost never the criterion for ready to bake

For the final proof the pages read agree with the owner. KAB: for a loaf pan, bake when the dough is
about 1 inch over the rim (a ruler, or the length of the thumb to its first knuckle), and for free-form
loaves use the poke test, because shaped dough rises outward as well as up. WB calls "double" a myth and
argues that for a half-sphere the volume doubles when the height grows by about 26 %, whereas doubling the
diameter multiplies the volume by eight. BP: the height or the spring. For bulk the picture is mixed: KAB's
professional guidance and its bagel, burger-bun and Chicago deep-dish recipes (the last from Bake from
Scratch) say "to double" or "nearly"; its pain de mie recipes say the bulk need not double ("puffy though
not necessarily doubled"); a straight-sided marked container is advised to read it. WB's sourdough table
asks for +50 to 75 %. The owner's two-stage pattern (double, degas, rise by half or double) is stated in
those terms by no page read; KAB says only that the second rise lets the dough expand again after shaping.

### C2. Pizza dough asked to triple

No pizza recipe or specification read says it. BP states that in a commercial final proof the dough expands
three or four times from a degassed piece at 35 to 37 °C (the page cites a reference not read). Covino 2023
(repository record): a free 250 g Neapolitan ball at 22 °C reaches 1.3 times its volume at 8 h, doubles at
16 h and triples at about 24 to 30 h, a measurement and not a criterion, with the paper's text and figure
differing on the time. So tripling is a description of what a long-fermented ball does, and no source reads
it as an end criterion.

### C3. Folds make the volume change unreadable

The mechanism is measured (Verheyen: the gas fraction after kneading is already 23 % with compressed yeast and
26 % with instant yeast, against 6 to 9 % unyeasted, and rises to 83 %), and the repository's contract and
ledger (L146) already state it. The pages read do not say it is impossible: KAB (Maurizio Leo) lists "volume
gain" among the signs at the end of a folded bulk and gives no ratio; WB's 2026 sourdough guide gives 50 to 75 %;
readers of WB describe a sample jar, a jam jar, a cylinder or a shot glass kept beside the bowl, one calling it
an "aliquot jar" (comments, not the authors). Verdict: qualified. Volume is read in practice with a marked
sample or container; it is one sign among several, not a ratio.

### C4. Cold holding after the dough is ready

KAB: pizza dough "will rise slowly as it chills" for 4 to 24 h (18 h for the whole wheat recipe); a long cold
bulk of 8 to 12 h, up to about 16 h, with the dough started at 75 to 80 °F and given 20 to 30 min on the counter
first; shaped, refrigerated bagels "will not need to warm up or rise any further" and cold fermentation gives
"a wide window of readiness". WB: 12 to 16 h at 2 to 4 °C as a standard retard, 16 to 24 h for a well-bulked
dough, a colder fridge allowing a longer one, and a pizza dough kept 24 h at about 6 °C with the yeast set for it.
How long a dough can be held is a property of dose and temperature (the repository's recipes add ceilings of 24, 48
and 72 h). That folds or degassing keep a held dough in check has no source read; what KAB does say is that after a
cold bulk the dough warms for 40 to 60 min until slightly puffy and a stretch and fold can help it along, which
is the opposite use of a fold. No source read says what degrades at the end of a cold window.

### C5. Over-expansion weakens the network; reworking

First half: KAB says a loaf that crests the rim and starts to overflow has a gluten structure "compromised and
weaker" and, baked as it is, shows a sunken centre, a curl of crust, a coarse crumb and a sour smell like over-ripe
fruit; its test loaf baked as it was "rose, fell and collapsed". Measured: Liu 2024 (frozen dough steamed bread,
outside the domain): product volume peaked at a proofed specific volume of 1.3 mL/g and fell steeply at 1.5 and
1.7, with a harder crumb; Rathnayake 2018 (a review): over-proofing thins cell walls, enlarges cells and hardens the
crumb; Alpers 2020: the gluten microstructure degrades during fermentation, chiefly because of the gas cells;
Verheyen 2015 (record): a critical gas volume makes the matrix unstable.

Second half: KAB (PJ Hamel, 2018): deflate, reshape, put back and bake when no more than an inch over the rim,
after about 20 minutes; the rescued loaf "rose a bit higher" with no flavour difference the author could taste;
active dry or instant yeast, not rapid-rise, and generally not sourdough. The 2014 page says the third rise goes
quickly and "probably won't be as high" because the yeast is tiring. BP's troubleshooting table gives causes and
remedies for the next batch (less yeast, less sugar, a cooler proof), not a rescue. So the owner's belief is
supported for a yeast sandwich loaf, qualified for rapid-rise yeast and sourdough, and "practically always" is not
stated by any source. What a late rework costs is not described; the only measurements near it are Covino's
compression work (29.96 J at 4 h, 7.65 J at 48 h) and Di Stasio's sugars (glucose 0.728 to 0.144 g per 100 g at 48 h),
which show that a 48 h dough has changed, not that a rework costs anything.

### C6. The Pullman loaf and the tin mark

Seven King Arthur lidded-tin recipes and one post: Honey-Oat Pain de Mie, rise "about 1 inch below the top of the
pan/lid, 60 to 90 minutes", with a warning that too much rise can pop the lid and too little leaves the pan unfilled;
the classic pain de mie in a 13 inch pan, "just below the lip, 45 minutes to 1 hour"; the whole wheat 13 inch recipe,
about 1/2 inch below the lip, 45 min; the smaller whole wheat, the domed centre about 3/4 inch below the lip,
60 to 90 min; the small white one, within about 1/2 inch of the top edge, about 60 min; Bee Keeper's, just below the lip;
Golden Spiced, within 1/2 inch of the top (the lid is pushed back to look). Bulk in these recipes: "puffy though not
necessarily doubled" or "risen noticeably". So a mark in the tin is real, and so is a bulk that need not double;
the gap is 1/2 inch (1.3 cm) to 1 inch (2.5 cm) depending on the recipe, so the owner's 1 cm is close to the low
end and not outside what one site prescribes. No page describes his no-knead procedure after a poolish over 24 to 48 h
or its smooth, less sticky bulk sign; BP's autolyse page gives a plausible mechanism (a long rest of flour and water
leaves a dough that "feels less sticky and very smooth", more extensible, less elastic). The mark is not universal:
open pan recipes use a height over the rim and free-form ones the poke test.

### C7. Bagels with a high dose and a continuous process

KAB (Andrew Janjigian, 2021): some recipes ferment in bulk and then shape, boil and bake "in quick succession";
others proof the shaped bagels for a long stretch, usually in the fridge, and most good bagels get a long cold proof.
Martin's Bagels: a 2 h rest with folds, then 8 to 12 h in the fridge, shape, rest 20 to 30 min, boil; Hamelman's
pâte fermentée bagels: the dough rises 2 h "to double in bulk, or at least expand considerably", shaped bagels
chill 2 h, then boil. The water-bagel recipe doubles in bulk, shapes and boils. A bagel baker's advice on the same
site is to pull the dough early, "definitely before they double in size", since over-proofed bagels deflate in the
water bath. So there is no rise target after shaping, as the owner says, but there is a ceiling, and the
continuous versions have a bulk rise first. No source read gives a dose of 2 %. The float test as a readiness test
of the dough was tested by KAB and rejected (see the catalogue); BP's float is the end of boiling.

### C8. Pizza ready is fermented and relaxed

The Association's disciplinare: maturation (an enzymatic breakdown of proteins and starch) with good coordination
of leavening gives a dough that is "plastic", extensible and little elastic, less tenacious and more digestible,
whereas the dough just out of the mixer is "little extensible and very elastic". KAB: more time in the fridge gives
"a more extensible dough"; a dough that snaps back needs a 15 minute rest; an overnight rest of preshaped balls in
the fridge relaxes the gluten. Covino 2023: the disc diameter after pressing rose from 21.5 cm at 0 h to 29.3 cm at
4 h and stayed 26 to 28 cm to 48 h, while the compression work fell from 29.96 J at 4 h to 7.65 J at 48 h. Second half:
BP's page on handling properties names a "too extensible" dough (slack, very low elasticity) and ties it to overmixing
(warm and sticky) and too much water, with sharp corners, too much pan flow and collapse from excessive oven spring;
nothing read calls an over-matured dough "too extensible" or sends it to the fridge.

### C9. Several acceptable maturity levels

The windows differ by recipe and by style: the Association 8 to 24 h at 23 °C with a dose table (1.5 g of fresh yeast
for 8 h, 0.3 g for 24 h); KAB 4 to 24 h (whole wheat 18 h); WB 24 h at about 6 °C; a 72-hour recipe record 24 h at
room temperature then 48 h cold then 3 to 4 h of proof; Roman scrocchiarella at most 48 h. These are the owner's
"several levels" as prescriptions. The Association's 2022 text and this repository's record of the 2024 regulations
(12 to 24 h) disagree on the minimum. Dark spots: no page read lists them as a sign of a matured pizza ball, and
open-access searches found nothing. The one page that names dark spots on dough (BP, "The Importance of Proofing")
ties them to a proof box saturated with water vapour, with sugars leaching to the surface, in commercial pan bread.

### C10. Gas, gluten and fermentation activity

Gluten: BP, an undermixed (cold) dough "proofs slower, low volume (poor gas retention as final development did not
occur)", an overmixed one is slack and proofs faster; KAB: gluten is the web that traps the gas. Activity: BP,
otherwise the pieces "are unable to relax sufficiently", with poor volume and a dense texture; KAB: if fermentation
slows too much, high-hydration doughs "literally fall without good fermentation". Measured: Iwaki 2020 and Vidal 2022
(a peak and a breakdown), Takata 2019 (abstract only: stress at the end of proofing correlates with gas retention and
loaf volume), Verheyen 2015 (the lowest dose needed three times the time and gave 3.1 against 3.5 mL/g). Verheyen 2014
qualifies the activity side: gas retention is limited by the gas amount and the matrix, not by the speed of production.

### C11. The poke test

The test is widely taught and its result is read in more than one way. KAB (four pages, one voice, 2014 to 2023): press
about an inch (the 2014 page: to the bed of the fingernail); a dent that stays means ready, one that pops back needs
more time; a fragile dough that collapses when poked is over-risen. WB: the dent that disappears completely means
under-proofed, popping halfway back means just right, staying entirely dented means over-proofed; right after shaping
the dent stays although the dough is not over-proofed, which one tells by feel. BP: the dough that springs back is
ready. KAB's sourdough contributor reads the feel instead: the interior should not feel dense and tight (if it does,
another 30 min), and very soft and weak or large bubbles under the surface mean bake now. The owner's reading agrees with
the KAB pages and disagrees with WB and BP. KAB says the test works "with dough in many forms" and calls it the best test
after the second rise. On cold dough a WB reader says the poke "doesn't help" because the dough always feels tough or
pliable (a comment); no page gives a test for chilled dough. Nothing read measures the test. A lead from Modernist Cuisine
(unread) is not used.

### C12. The poolish that smells of alcohol

KAB (professional reference, author not stated): a ripe poolish has its surface covered with small bubbles, CO2
bubbles breaking through, "a pleasing aroma that has a perceptible tang" and a slight tang when tasted; a stiff
preferment is ripe when fully risen and just beginning to recede in the centre; one past ripeness gives bread lacking
luster, volume and flavour. BP: at its peak a poolish's volume starts to fall back and the surface wrinkles; fermenting too
long breaks down the gluten and raises acidity and alcohol to unacceptable levels. WB (replies of the authors in the
comments): an over-ripe poolish turns thin and then soupy and leaves a rim where it rose and fell; a collapsed one has weaker
gluten, and as a rescue the final dough can use less water; to gain time use less yeast or the fridge. A reader reports a stronger
alcohol smell after a poolish had tripled and fallen back to double; the reply addresses the weak gluten and not the smell.
No page names a strong alcohol smell as the cue; chilling is named, for a poolish that needs more time, in a comment reply.
Di Stasio 2025 shows ethanol accumulating steadily in a long yeast dough (a pizza dough, not a poolish), which makes a
stronger smell with time plausible.

## The three most contested criteria

**1. The result of the poke test.** KAB: a dent that stays = ready; a dent that pops back = more time; a fragile
dough that collapses when poked = over-risen (four pages, one voice). WB: a dent that stays entirely = over-proofed;
halfway back = right; fully back = under. BP: springs back = ready. KAB's sourdough contributor: a tight interior needs
30 more minutes, a very soft and weak one is ready. The sources disagree on the same observation; no measurement was
found that settles it (open-access searches for poke and indentation tests found nothing). What is common: the dough
should be elastic and light, not dense or fragile; the test is read on a dough at room temperature after shaping; and no
source gives a test for a chilled dough.

**2. "Doubled in size".** For: KAB's advice for bulk in a marked straight-sided container and its recipes "to double";
BP's three-to-four-fold expansion of a degassed commercial piece (a description). Against or qualifying: WB's ambiguity
argument and its +50 to 75 % for a sourdough bulk; KAB's own pan recipes ("not necessarily doubled") and "pull before they
double" for bagels. For the final proof the pages use a mark (an inch over a pan rim, 1/2 to 1 inch below a lid), the poke, or
a spring test, not a ratio.

**3. How far a past-peak dough can be saved.** For: KAB's deflate, reshape and re-rise (yeast loaves, a third rise of about 20
minutes, no flavour difference tasted by the author); WB's less water in the final dough for a collapsed poolish. Limits: the same
KAB site says the third rise is lower, not for rapid-rise yeast, generally not for sourdough; BP says an overmixed dough in
breakdown "is not salvageable" (a different failure: gluten, not gas); BP and the BAKERguide give only next-batch remedies. The
owner's "practically always" has support only for the first case.

## The catalogue of signs, by stage

**Scope.** The owner asked for every sign, by every sense, with what it means and what to do about it, and for
negative signs recorded as carefully as "ready" signs. The catalogue holds what was read, one row per sign. Columns:
the id; the sense and the sign (sight, touch, smell, taste, time, or a named instrument; **no sound sign appears in any page
read**); what it indicates; the action the source gives and its consequence ("Proceed." means the source only says to
go on; "None stated." means it gives no action); when the sign is unreliable according to a source; and the sources with the
level of the register entry (`level X`) and any measured backing. A row of the owner's with no page behind it says "no source read yet".
Reader comments and replies in comments are marked and are always level C. An action at level C is never to be shown to a baker
as guidance (the register's ceiling of use).

## Synthesis

- **Readiness is a conjunction, and it differs by step and by recipe.** The pages show at least six kinds of
  condition: the dough temperature at the start; gas made (a volume or a time); gluten developed (smooth, windowpane,
  a film); relaxation (the pull back, a rest); a state at the moment (a mark in the tin, a poke, a core temperature);
  and a ceiling (do not over-proof a bagel, pull the dough before it doubles). No page gives one universal criterion,
  and the same word means different things in different recipes (a bulk "to double" in one recipe, "need not double" in
  the next recipe of the same site).
- **What a home baker can observe** in these sources: time; a thermometer; a marked straight-sided container or a
  small sample jar; a ruler or the thumb against a pan rim; touch (smooth, elastic, pulls back, a dent, a tight or soft
  interior); sight (domed, bubbles, jiggle, a wrinkled surface, a nearly transparent dome); smell and taste (a pleasing
  tang). **Sound appears nowhere.** Gas fraction, pH, ethanol and torque are laboratory measures.
- **Which model each criterion needs.**

  | Kind of criterion                                                                                                   | Needs                                                                         |
  | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | A volume or time criterion where nothing has degassed the dough; a time at a stated temperature; a cold window      | the timing model (dose and temperature history)                               |
  | Gluten state, relaxation and extensibility, the over-proofing curve, a tight or soft interior                       | the behavior model, if predicted; otherwise the baker's observation           |
  | A mark in the tin, a dough or core temperature, a process step ("boil from the fridge"), a ceiling stated as a size | neither: a check against the recipe                                           |
  | A smell, a taste, a poke, a jiggle                                                                                  | neither: the baker's observation, with the disagreement on the poke left open |

- **What the contract and code already model.** `volumeCueValid` (in `packages/core/src/domain/bake/thermal.ts`;
  `liveDoughForecast` is in `forecast.ts`) turns the "about doubled" cue off once a fold, degas, preshape or shape has
  been recorded since the step began, and the other cues the contract names are the dome, bubbles, jiggle, poke and feel
  (L146). L145 puts the baker's cues first and L21 bans a computed "doubled" target. Issues #243 (the cues, not yet on a
  screen), #246 (wording) and #287 hold the open work. What this catalogue shows is missing from them: signs for the stages
  before the last proof (preferment ripeness, end of mixing, relaxation), a mark in the tin and a pan-rim gap as cue kinds,
  a core or dough temperature as a cue, a ceiling cue ("pull before doubling"), negative signs with their remedies, a sample
  jar as a way to read volume, and the disagreement on what the poke test shows, which the interface cannot settle by a rule.
- **Proposed direction, not accepted.** The ADR described at the top (proposed, not accepted) puts the criterion in the
  step and the baker, and the knowledge in a codified body. It requires that a trade term never be shown without a
  definition; this catalogue uses terms (poolish, biga, appretto, staglio, dough development time, autolyse, windowpane,
  bulk, preshape, bench rest, proof, retard, poke test, aliquot jar) that have no definition entry yet.

## What this implies, as questions for the design

These are questions, not decisions.

1. **Kinds of end criterion.** A step could have: a volume ratio since the last degassing (timing model, with the
   volume-cue rule); a physical mark or a gap to a rim (neither model); a handling criterion such as relaxed enough or a
   poke result (behavior model if predicted, a baker's observation if not); a measured quantity such as a dough or core
   temperature (the thermal sub-model); a ceiling ("pull before doubling"); no rise criterion, only a process step
   (neither); a maturity window with a start and an end (timing model for the age, behavior model for what the window
   means). Should a step carry several, as a conjunction?
2. **How to hold a disagreement on the same sign.** The poke test has three or four readings in the sources read.
   Should the product present the test with its observation described, and leave the reading to the recipe's author or the
   baker, rather than a rule?
3. **Whether a "remedy" field is allowed**, since the only rescues read (deflate, reshape, rise again; less water for a
   collapsed poolish) each rest on one organisation or one comment reply, with limits (yeast type, sourdough).
4. **Where an over- or under-proofed warning belongs**, given that the one measured curve (Liu 2024) is for another product
   and the repository has no home measurement of it, and that a dough in a pan may be pushed further than a free-standing one.
5. **What to do with a lead** (a search summary of a page that could not be read), and with reader comments, which are
   the only source for the sample jar and for the poke on chilled dough.
6. **Which reference works to read next**, and whether the owner can supply the chapters listed below.

## Proposal: the data shape for a catalogue of signs

A Proposal, not a contract. One record per sign, so that it can later be codified and shown at the right step of a recipe:

```text
Sign: <language-neutral id, e.g. proof.poke-indent-stays>
- Stage: preferment | mix | autolyse | bulk | rest | proof | cold | boil | after
- Sense: sight | touch | smell | sound | taste | measurement (named instrument)
- Sign: what is perceived, and the gesture to perceive it (no wording for the interface)
- Indicated state: under | right | over | other, and the dough property: gas produced, gas retained,
  gluten development, relaxation, temperature, acidity
- Action: use now | wait | fold | degas | re-ball | chill | warm | shorten next step | bake at once |
  discard | none stated; each with the source that gives it
- Consequence of ignoring: as stated, or none stated
- Valid when / unreliable when: styles, hydration, temperature, handling since the step began
- Sources: register entry ids, with their levels; disagreeing entries listed, never averaged
- Measured backing: scientific source ids, if any, with their roles
- Checked: UTC date, and a role
```

**Relation to existing templates.** The record is a **specialisation** of the behavior statement of
[behavior-model-foundations.md](behavior-model-foundations.md#4c-the-form-of-a-behavior-statement): its Property is a readiness
state perceived by a sense, its Driver or Condition is the step and what has happened to the dough, and it adds the sense, the action
and the consequence that a statement about direction and size does not carry. It is a **sibling** of the craft-register entry of
[craft-knowledge.md](craft-knowledge.md#entry-template): the register entry is the provenance (one source's claim, graded), and a sign
record cites several entries; neither template changes. A sign's recorded `action` is craft knowledge and takes the register's ceilings of
use: a level C action is never shown to a baker as guidance. A disagreement (as on the poke test) is two sign records on the same
observation, each with its sources, and no merged record.
