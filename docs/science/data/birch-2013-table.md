## Paper II: protocol (LWT 50:481, section 2.3; thesis PDF p. 77)

- 300 g flour (adjusted to 14 % moisture; measured flour moisture 11.7 to 11.9 %), **190 mL water at 30 °C**, 4 g saccharose, 4 g NaCl, and 6, 12 or 18 g yeast (20, 40, 60 g per kg of flour). The thesis (Table 1, p. 4) gives water 63.3 %, salt 1.3 % and sugar 1.3 % of flour for Paper II.
- Yeast: pressed baker's yeast "Malteserkors" (Lallemand, Grenå), unwashed, dry matter 28 %. The paper prints a cell count of 1.44 x 10^13 cells per g; Paper I (Table 1) and the thesis give (1.44 +- 0.17) x 10^10 CFU per g for the same product, a factor 1000 apart with the same mantissa (an exponent misprint in one of them; unresolved). Doses in g per kg of flour are unaffected. The paper states that "preliminary tests showed that the yeast cell density was the same in the dough immediately after mixing of the dough and in the end of the fermentation period" (no data shown).
- **Mixed 19 min in a baking machine (Funai FAB-100).** Two 235 g pieces from each mix went each into a 1 L beaker sealed with aluminium foil and were left in an incubator at 5, 15 or 35 °C. Fermentation was stopped when the dough reached **8 cm in total height**; the time for each cell was found by "preliminary tests ... at each yeast level and fermentation temperature", with the height monitored by a web camera (Table 1). Baked at 130 °C for 31 min with steam.
- **Dough temperature after the machine mix is not reported**, nor the starting height of the dough, nor a dough temperature during the run. The temperature in the table is the incubator's.
- The predefined height was "determined by baking experiments and subjective evaluation of the crumb structure" (thesis p. 9): a baker-type end point of a fully developed dough, not a fraction of a maximum.

## Paper III: seven commercial yeasts at equal cell count (Table 2 of the paper, Table 5 of the thesis)

Dough as Paper II except 185 mL water, 25 °C, **2.88 x 10^11 CFU per kg of flour** (the thesis and Paper I; Paper III prints 2.88 x 10^14, the same exponent misprint), end point 10 cm total height, from preliminary tests with a video camera. Times in min: Malteserkors 100 +- 7, Skærtoftmølle 71 +- 5, Rapunzel 65 +- 3, Sema 60 +- 8, Zymarom 46 +- 2, Bruggeman 40 +- 5, l'Hirondelle 40 +- 6. The "+-" is not defined in the caption. At that cell count the mass doses are 20, 31, 40, 22, 37, 39 and 40 g per kg of flour (Paper I, Table 1; Paper III Table 1 gives the same masses): the products differ by a factor 2.5 in time at equal cells, and by a factor 2 in the mass of product needed for those cells.

## Paper I: what it contains (J. Cereal Sci. 58:318-323; thesis PDF p. 62-72; summarised in thesis section 2.4, p. 11-15)

- Seven compressed yeasts, three doses of 2.88, 5.76 and 8.64 x 10^11 CFU per kg of flour (20 to 120 g per kg depending on the product, Table 1), four temperatures (5, 15, 25, 35 °C): 84 conditions. Dough: 300 g flour (adjusted to 14 %; measured 12.6 to 13.2 %), **185 mL water (30 °C)**, 4 g saccharose, 4 g NaCl, mixed 19 min in the same machine; 235 g in a 1 L beaker under foil.
- Height of the highest point of the dough read from web-camera pictures every 1 to 15 min. Model `h = b0 + b1 (1 - exp(-k t))`, fitted by nonlinear regression for each condition. The "height for optimally developed dough" is 90 mm at 5 °C and 100 mm at 15, 25 and 35 °C, from pre-trials that baked doughs of several heights and judged the crumb (results not shown).
- **Replication, as the paper says it** (section 2.4, p. 3 of the paper): "The dough heights were monitored as duplicates from the same dough that was divided in two samples of 235 g dough." The duplicates are portions of one mix, not independent mixes. Table 2 gives "mean kinetic rate constants of duplicates" with one or two significant digits (for example, read from the text layer of the table, not transcribed, 0.0005, 0.005, 0.01 min^-1 for Malteserkors at 5, 15, 25 °C at the lowest dose); the mean coefficient of variation of the rate constants over all samples is 17.1 % (p. 5). Figure 1 reports N = 18 samples at 5 °C and N = 56 at 15, 25 and 35 °C, which is not 21 and 63; the text read does not explain it. Two doughs (l'Hirondelle and Sema, 25 °C, lowest dose) were dropped for curve shape.
- Three yeasts (Malteserkors, Sema, Skærtoftmølle) "were not able to reach the optimal dough height at 5 °C" (thesis p. 15; the window is not stated), whereas Paper II reached 8 cm at 20 g/kg of Malteserkors at 5 °C in 21 h: the end points (8 against 9 cm) and the flour moisture differ.
- Not read at cell level: the rate-constant cells themselves (the tables are rotated in the thesis; only their structure is recorded here), Figure 7, and the ANOVA letters. The rate `k` is a height-fit constant, not a CO2 constant.

## What it does and does not show

- It is the only other dough timing matrix of the ledger at or below 8 °C with several doses, and a second Copenhagen matrix beside Izzreen. Same laboratory group and the same 2/4/6 % fresh-yeast design (Izzreen's mixing procedure was not read here), but another flour (white), another vessel (1 L beaker), other temperatures (5, 15, 35 °C) and another end point (8 cm).
- The dough leaves a 19-min machine mix made with 30 °C water and then cools or warms in the incubator; its temperature is not reported. The 5 °C row (21 h, 3.5 h, 3 h) is therefore not a cold-kinetics measurement.
- The apparent dose exponents of the nine cells, by both estimators, are in `fermentation-model.md`, section 5 (DERIVED).
- It gives no dough-temperature history, no replication of the times, and doses of 2 to 6 % (above the 3 % gate for 4 and 6 %).
